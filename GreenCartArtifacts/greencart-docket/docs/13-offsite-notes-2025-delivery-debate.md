# Leadership offsite notes — the four-hour window debate

Honest notes from the November 2025 offsite session on delivery windows. Written up so the reasoning survives, not just the decision. If this question comes back — and it will — start from here instead of re-arguing from zero.

## The proposal

Indore operations proposed moving Indore from the two-hour promise to a **four-hour window**. Their case: Indore's rider pool is small (see the honest parts of the [Indore BRD](14-brd-indore-expansion.md)), evening lateness there is the worst of the three cities, and a wider window would let us batch more orders per rider and cut delivery cost meaningfully.

## The case against

<!-- frag: offsite-2h-brand-decision | weight: critical | topics: delivery, promise, strategy -->
The counter-argument, which carried the room: **two hours is not an operations setting, it is the product.** Customer research in all three cities keeps saying the same thing — people choose GreenCart to avoid planning; four hours means planning your afternoon around a delivery, which is the exact thing we sell freedom from. Pune and Jaipur hit the promise fine, so this is an Indore capacity problem wearing a company-wide costume. Decision: **the two-hour promise stays, in all cities.** Indore gets capacity fixes instead — rider hiring push, better batching during the 5–8 pm peak. Revisit only if Indore lateness is still bad after those land, and any future change must be framed as a scheduled-delivery *option next to* the two-hour promise, never a replacement of it.
<!-- /frag -->
