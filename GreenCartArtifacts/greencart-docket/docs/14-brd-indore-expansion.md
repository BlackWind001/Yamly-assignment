# Business case — expansion to Indore

Prepared August 2024 to support the decision to launch a third city. Approved September 2024; Indore launched January 2025. Most of this document is market context for the approval meeting. The parts that still matter day-to-day are in the "Operating realities" section near the end.

## Market opportunity

Indore is among the fastest-growing tier-2 cities in central India, with a large young working population and high smartphone use. Online grocery adoption in comparable cities has grown steadily year over year. We estimate a serviceable market of several hundred thousand households in the neighborhoods we would launch in, concentrated in a compact urban core that suits our two-hour model.

## Competition

One national quick-commerce player operates in Indore with a limited catalog focused on convenience items. Traditional store delivery (call the shop, boy on a bicycle) remains common and is the true competitor for full-basket grocery. We believe our full-catalog, partner-store model is differentiated from both, as it was in Jaipur.

## Financial projection

We project reaching contribution breakeven in Indore within 18 months of launch, driven by order density in the core neighborhoods. Projected order volumes, basket sizes, and delivery cost curves are in the finance annex (not in this folder). These projections assume delivery economics comparable to Jaipur's second year.

## Launch plan

Phase 1: 20 partner stores across four central neighborhoods. Phase 2: widen to 45 stores within six months. Picker hiring and training runs four weeks ahead of launch, using the Jaipur playbook.

## Operating realities

The parts of this document people actually come back for.

<!-- frag: brd-store-hours-9pm | weight: critical | topics: delivery, indore -->
**Indore partner stores close at 9 pm** — earlier than Pune (11 pm) and Jaipur (10 pm). Since the two-hour promise needs a store open through picking, the last order slot in Indore is meaningfully earlier than in the other cities. Anything that plans capacity, promises, or campaigns "company-wide by hour of day" and ignores this will quietly be wrong for Indore. Late-evening demand that Pune serves simply cannot exist in Indore under current store hours.
<!-- /frag -->

<!-- frag: brd-rider-pool | weight: helpful | topics: delivery, indore -->
**The rider labor pool in Indore is smaller and more seasonal** than in the other two cities; many riders return to family farms around major festivals and harvests. Rider capacity planning for Indore cannot simply copy the Pune model, and hiring pushes need to be timed around these seasonal dips rather than fighting them.
<!-- /frag -->

<!-- frag: brd-festival-spike | weight: helpful | topics: demand, indore -->
**Festival demand spikes in Indore are sharper than we see elsewhere** — order volume during the main festival weeks can run several times a normal week, while rider availability (see above) simultaneously dips. The launch plan deliberately avoided the festival season for this reason, and ongoing capacity planning should treat festival weeks in Indore as their own special case.
<!-- /frag -->

## Risks considered

Store system integration in Indore involves a billing vendor we have not integrated before (this later mattered — see the [stale stock incident](06-incident-2025-02-stale-stock.md)). Rider supply risk as above. Competitive response considered low for full-basket grocery.
